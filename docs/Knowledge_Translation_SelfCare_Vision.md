## 1. THE PROBLEM: RESEARCH WITHOUT REACH

Across Malawi — and Sub-Saharan Africa — health research is produced constantly. Studies on HIV adherence in Blantyre, maternal outcomes in Nsanje, childhood nutrition in Neno. The findings are published in journals, presented at conferences, archived in repositories. Then they disappear.

**The communities who were the subjects of the research never hear the results.**

A mother in TA Ndamera does not know that a study conducted in her own village found that early ANC attendance reduces preterm birth by 40%. A father in Neno does not know that research in his district demonstrated that a specific feeding practice cuts stunting rates by half. The knowledge exists. The people who need it most do not.

This is not a technology problem. It is a **translation problem** — and a **trust problem**.

> *"The care is the root. People suffer when they do not know. We are the gatekeeper ensuring information passes through from those that hold it to those that need to use it in order for them to better take care of themselves."*

---

## 2. THE VISION: SAMALA-AI

**SAMALA-AI** is the fourth engine of the Zisamale platform. Where MAMA-AI collects data, ULALO-AI predicts threats, and CHUMA-AI optimizes resources, **SAMALA-AI translates existing knowledge into life-changing action.**

"Kuzisamala" — to take care of yourself — is the platform's namesake. SAMALA-AI makes that literal.

### Core Purpose

1. **Find** existing research and studies relevant to communities in Malawi
2. **Synthesize** that research into clear, actionable, culturally appropriate communication assets
3. **Deliver** those assets back to the communities where the research was conducted
4. **Measure** impact on knowledge, behavior, and health outcomes
5. **Empower** individuals with self-care information they can act on immediately

## 4. SCENARIO ARCHETYPES

### Scenario A: Research-to-Community Translation

**The Setup:** A researcher from the University of Malawi College of Medicine publishes a study on HIV treatment adherence in Zomba. The study finds that patients who receive weekly SMS reminders and peer support have 35% better viral suppression rates.

**The Gap:** The patients in Zomba who participated in the study never see the results. The clinics do not adopt the intervention. The knowledge dies in the journal.

**SAMALA-AI Action:**
1. **Discover:** The SAMALA ingestion pipeline flags the study via PubMed alert
2. **Validate:** A Zisamale research liaison contacts the author for consent and clarification
3. **Synthesize:** The LLM engine extracts key findings into a CHW briefing card:
   > *"A study in Zomba found that people on HIV medicine who got weekly text reminders and talked with a support friend were 35% more likely to have strong health. This means: (1) set a weekly reminder, (2) find a treatment buddy, (3) tell your CHW if you miss a dose."*
4. **Adapt:** Assets created:
   - 3-minute Chichewa video drama showing a couple using the buddy system
   - CHW flipbook for home visits
   - SMS template for clinic staff
   - Community poster for waiting areas
5. **Deploy:** Grace (CHW) receives the asset pack in her app. During her next visit to an HIV-positive household, she shares the video on her phone and helps the patient set a phone reminder.
6. **Measure:** 3-month follow-up shows 28% improvement in self-reported adherence in catchment areas where SAMALA deployed the asset.

### Scenario B: Self-Care Information Library

**The Setup:** A pregnant woman in Nsanje, 7 months along, has swollen feet and a headache. She does not know these are warning signs of pre-eclampsia. Her nearest clinic is 12km away. She waits.

**SAMALA-AI Action:**
1. **Grace's App:** During a routine visit, Grace opens the "Kuzisamala" section of her app. She finds a pre-eclampsia self-care guide.
2. **The Guide:** Visual, step-by-step:
   - "If your feet swell and you have a headache, this is serious."
   - "Lie on your left side. Drink water."
   - "Go to the clinic TODAY. Do not wait."
   - "While you travel: keep sitting, do not walk far."
3. **Audio Mode:** Grace plays the Chichewa audio narration for the woman, who cannot read.
4. **Follow-up:** The app prompts Grace to check on this woman in 48 hours. If she did not go to clinic, escalate to supervisor.

### Scenario C: Community Health Education Program

**The Setup:** Neno District has high rates of childhood diarrhea during the rainy season. Research from the district hospital identifies handwashing with soap and safe water storage as the two most effective prevention measures.

**SAMALA-AI Action:**
1. **Synthesize:** The research is turned into a "Community Health Talk" module — a 15-minute structured session any CHW can deliver.
2. **Assets:**
   - Picture cards showing dirty water → clean water
   - A song in Chichewa about handwashing (composed by local musicians)
   - A simple tippy-tap construction diagram
   - A pledge card: "My family will wash hands before eating"
3. **Deploy:** Grace delivers the health talk at a village under-the-tree meeting. Mothers take pledge cards home. CHWs track adoption via follow-up visits.
4. **Measure:** Diarrhea cases in Neno drop 18% the following rainy season.

## 5. THE SAMALA-AI ENGINE ARCHITECTURE

### 5.1 Components

| Component | Function | Technology |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| **Research Ingestion Service** | Scans PubMed, AJOL, WHO IRIS, local repositories for Malawi-relevant research | Python + PubMed API + RSS feeds |
| **Synthesis Engine** | Summarizes research into plain language; extracts actionable takeaways | LLM (local or API) + prompt engineering |
| **Asset Factory** | Generates communication assets from synthesized content | Template engine + video pipeline + Canva API |
| **Community Mapper** | Matches research locations to actual communities and CHW catchments | PostGIS + GPS + TA boundary data |
| **Deployment Tracker** | Logs which assets were delivered where, by which CHW | SQLite on-device + PostgreSQL cloud |
| **Impact Evaluator** | Measures knowledge retention and behavior change | Survey engine + CHW reporting + DHIS2 indicators |
| **Self-Care Library** | Offline-first repository of health guides, symptom checkers, preventive care info | Structured content + LLM Q&A |

## 6. USER EXPERIENCES

### 6.1 Grace (CHW): The Knowledge Broker

Grace's app now has a **"Kuzisamala"** tab alongside her patient list. Here she finds:

- **Today's Health Message:** A one-sentence takeaway from recent research, in Chichewa. *"A study in Neno found that washing hands with soap stops diarrhea in children."*
- **Self-Care Guides:** Searchable by symptom or topic. Each guide has pictures, audio narration, and simple steps.
- **Asset Library:** Downloaded videos, posters, and flipbooks she can share during visits.
- **Community Talk Tracker:** Templates for delivering group health talks, with attendance recording.

**During a visit:**
1. Grace identifies a pregnant woman with warning signs
2. She opens the pre-eclampsia self-care guide
3. Plays the Chichewa audio explanation
4. Shows the illustrated "what to do" steps
5. The app auto-logs that this household received this guide
6. Grace schedules a follow-up visit in 48 hours

### 6.2 The Community: Receiving Knowledge

**At a village meeting:**
- Grace plays a 3-minute video drama on her phone (via Bluetooth speaker)
- Community members see themselves represented — the actors speak Chichewa, the setting looks like their village
- After the video, Grace uses picture cards to reinforce key messages
- Mothers sign pledge cards (thumbprints for non-readers)
- The meeting is logged: date, topic, attendees, follow-up plan

**At home:**
- A mother refers to a poster on her wall: "5 signs your child needs immediate care"
- She recognizes fast breathing in her child and goes to the clinic
- The poster saved a life

### 6.3 DHO Dashboard: Knowledge Translation Tracker

The DHO dashboard gains a **"SAMALA"** tab:

- **Research Inventory:** All studies relevant to the district, with synthesis status
- **Asset Deployment Map:** Which communities have received which knowledge assets
- **Knowledge Coverage:** % of catchment areas reached with specific health topics
- **Impact Metrics:** Pre/post knowledge scores, behavior change rates, health outcome trends
- **Community Feedback:** Qualitative feedback from CHWs and community members

### 6.4 MoHP Dashboard: National Knowledge Strategy

The ministerial dashboard gains:

- **National Research Uptake Score:** How much published research is being translated vs. sitting dormant
- **Topic Gap Analysis:** Which health topics have little community-facing knowledge (e.g., "mental health: 3 studies, 0 assets deployed")
- **Funding Tracker:** Budget allocation for knowledge translation activities
- **Policy Brief Generator:** Auto-generates policy briefs from synthesized research with cost-benefit analysis

## 7. CONTENT PRINCIPLES

### 7.1 The 5 Rules of Translation

1. **Chichewa First:** Primary language is always Chichewa. English is secondary, smaller, lighter.
2. **One Idea per Asset:** A poster says one thing. A video teaches one behavior. Do not overload.
3. **Show, Don't Tell:** Pictures, diagrams, and dramas beat text every time.
4. **Actionable:** Every asset must end with "Do this now." Not "Researchers found that..." but "Wash your hands before eating."
5. **Dignified:** Never show suffering for shock value. Show strength, care, and community.

### 7.2 Accessibility Standards

- All videos have Chichewa audio narration (not just subtitles)
- All text assets have audio versions
- Picture-based guides for low-literacy users
- Large print versions for low-vision users
- SMS alternatives for households without smartphones

### 7.3 Cultural Safety

- All content reviewed by local clinical advisors
- Community pre-testing before wide deployment
- Researcher consent required before translating their work
- Community advisory boards approve asset content
- No stigmatizing language (e.g., "HIV victim" → "person living with HIV")

## 10. ETHICAL FRAMEWORK

### 10.1 Researcher Rights
- Researchers must give explicit consent before their work is translated
- Researchers are credited on every asset
- Researchers can review and approve synthesized content before deployment
- Researchers receive impact reports showing how their work changed communities

### 10.2 Community Rights
- Communities have the right to receive findings from research conducted on them
- Communities can opt out of receiving knowledge assets
- Community feedback directly shapes future asset design
- Community advisory boards have veto power over content

### 10.3 CHW Rights
- CHWs are trained, not burdened. Knowledge translation is part of their role, not an add-on.
- CHWs are compensated for additional training and community talk delivery
- CHW feedback on asset usability is actively sought and acted upon
